# PredicateImpact Claim--Evidence Matrix

This matrix governs the current predicate-level regression-testing paper.
Claims from the archived recovery paper are separate and may not be imported
as positive evidence here.

| Claim | Direct evidence | Permitted wording | Boundary |
|---|---|---|---|
| Predicate-level coverage improves early detection over skill-level association | R23: 64/64 versus exact family-random expectation 0.5561, lift 0.4439, one-sided conditional p=1.65e-19 | On the frozen deletion benchmark, predicate coverage substantially improves three-test-budget detection | Conditional on killable frozen mutations; not population inference |
| The result transfers beyond deletion | R24 disjoint roster: 115/115, expected 0.5567, lift 0.4433, p=8.29e-35 | The gain reproduces for deletion, same-kind substitution, and adjacent-order swap | R24 was designed after R23; it confirms operator transfer, not an untouched replication of the whole research process |

| Coverage granularity causes the observed method contrast | Exact predicate-coverage-set random also detects every killable change | Fine-grained impact-set identification is the supported component | No evidence that downstream-span/cost tie-breaking improves the main endpoint |
| Predicate-level selection reduces replay work | Three tests versus every eligible test in the changed family: 75.45% and 71.32% fewer executions | The policy can reduce early regression executions on these suites | This is an execution-count comparison, not measured LLM-token or production latency savings |

## Prohibited Claims

- Do not call the 1.000 detection rate a universal fault-detection guarantee.
- Do not omit the conditioning on killable mutations.
- Do not claim that within-coverage ranking beat coverage-set random.
- Do not call R25 commits faulty or treat file edits as independent observations.
- Do not claim automatic semantic diff localization for arbitrary prose skills.
- Do not relabel the R26 noise grid as preregistered or outcome-blind.
- Do not use the failed recovery study as efficacy evidence for PredicateImpact.
